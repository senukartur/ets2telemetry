## Post-review fix (PR #53 advisory review)

The advisory review of PR #53 found that `odometerStep` misclassified high-mileage odometers. `truck.odometer` is an SDK float32 in km: from 2^19 km one quantized step is 62.5 m and from 2^20 km 125 m, above the fixed 55 m threshold of a 20 Hz step. Every increment of such a truck was therefore an `odometer-jump`. The jump margin is now the larger of 50 m and two float32 quanta of the larger reading (`odometerJumpMargin`, using the exact `float32Quantum`). The regression check is unchanged because round-to-nearest float32 is monotonic: quantization alone cannot turn a non-decreasing odometer into a decrease, and a test keeps a one-quantum decrease as `odometer-regressed`. The [field rules](../live-telemetry-fields.md#step-primitives-reg-008) describe the margin.

New tests in `telemetry-core-derived.test.ts`: exact quanta at and around 2^19 and 2^20 km; a 600,000 km odometer with a +62.5 m step at 20 Hz spacing is continuous; a teleport of a high-mileage truck is still a jump; the 20 Hz thresholds of 130 m at 2^19 km and 255 m at 2^20 km (and the unchanged 55 m at low mileage) on both sides; and the one-quantum regression.

RED: with the fixed margin restored in `odometerStep` (helpers kept), 2 of 25 derived tests failed: the 600,000 km step and the 2^19/2^20 boundary cases. The source was restored afterwards.

#### Telemetry smoke and outside input

On `27f95fc` the verifier ran the development `telemetry` smoke twice. Run 1 failed and run 2 passed. In run 1, `runtime-smoke.mjs` timed out at line 141 waiting for the viewport-bound `Speed: 72.0 km/h` after the renderer stall. No code in the application or the smoke scrolls, focuses or sends input, so the likely cause is outside mouse input on the shared host; Chromium scrolls on its compositor thread even while the renderer's main thread is stalled. The dashboard already overflowed the 800 × 500 smoke window before ET-010b (the application status and display units cards lie below the first card). The new card makes it much taller, so outside scrolling can move further and push the legacy viewport-bound lines out of view (plan risk B6).

Mitigation in `570d3f7`: in the `telemetry` scenario only, the journey calls `scrollTo(0, 0)` in the dashboard right before each wait that requires the status, speed and fuel lines inside the viewport: each current observation, the held-stale and disconnected live checks, and the post-stall recovery. The predicates, deadlines, overlay and lifecycle observations and the receipt are unchanged, so the legacy lines must still be inside the top viewport of the unchanged window. Scrolling that happens before a wait, as in the failed run, no longer matters. Outside input during a wait itself can still move the page; making the window ignore mouse input or enlarging the emulated viewport would remove that too, but would change the window's state or make the viewport check trivial, so neither was applied. This mitigation has not been tested against real outside input.
